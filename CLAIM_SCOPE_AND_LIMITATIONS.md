# Claim scope and limitations

- The problem is one pair of language expressions over a finite nonempty alphabet and a rational threshold $p\ge0$. Constants are finite. Operators are Boolean operations and uniformly left concatenation by a finite constant. Word reversal gives the uniformly right-sided version.
- The assignment to each variable is finite, even when the evaluated expressions are infinite. Complement is relative to $\Sigma^*$. A closed term can occur as an equation side. The grammar requires an explicit finite constant as a concatenation factor.
- A finite assignment with $d_2\le p$ exists for $p>V_0$, where $V_0$ is the exact unrestricted infimum. At $p=V_0$ the algorithm tests finite attainment. The strict question depends only on comparison with the infimum. Exact finite solvability at $p=0$ has earlier algorithms.
- The proof constructs a finite witness on acceptance and applies to any finite number of variables. The two graphs in the explorer are exact worked examples.
- Other metrics, unrestricted concatenation and concept-distance questions with extra constraints lie outside this claim. The result completes the optimal-transition construction proposed by Marantidis. Historical priority remains open.
